# Apa Kata Mereka — Research and Attribution Protocol

## Purpose

This page will present a small, clearly sourced reading list of public perspectives about ULM, the Faculty of Law, or—where publicly available—the criminal-law learning experience. It is **not** a rating aggregator and does not claim to represent student consensus.

## Inclusion standard

An entry must originate from a publicly accessible, individually attributable post, review, comment, interview, or discussion that identifies the relevant ULM context. The original URL must resolve publicly at the time of verification. The entry must provide a substantive first-hand observation or a clearly personal viewpoint, rather than a generic promotional statement, search-result summary, or unverified repost.

## Writing standard

Every selected item will be paraphrased in original wording in two to three sentences. A direct quotation, if used at all, must be substantially below 20 words, accurately transcribed, visually marked as a quotation, and accompanied by the source link. The site will not reproduce a complete review, comment thread, rating score, user handle, profile photo, or any private contact information.

## Scope labels

Every card will state whether its scope is **Faculty of Law**, **ULM generally**, or **legal-study context**. The page will include the author’s publicly evident relationship only at the level warranted by the source (for example, “student perspective” or “faculty perspective”), never infer a role from a name.

## Candidate source categories

The research pass will assess: (1) Reddit threads and comments, (2) Quora answers, (3) individually authored public blog or forum posts, (4) public Google Maps reviews when a direct review URL is accessible, and (5) public faculty, student, or alumni writing on recognised platforms. Content behind login, content limited to search-result snippets, platforms that block public reading, and accounts whose identity or relevance cannot be checked will be excluded.

## Presentation rules

Cards will use a consistent River Margin layout while applying the selected platform’s publicly recognised accent color and a compact platform mark. Each card will include a prominent **Read the full post on [Platform]** action, the source date if visible, and a source-scope tag. The page will show an explicit notice that perspectives are selected public viewpoints, not endorsements by Saku Hukum ULM or ULM.

Page two of the same independent article identifies Prof. Mirza Satria Buana as a Guru Besar in Constitutional and Human Rights Law at FH ULM and says he was one of the lecturers most respected and liked by FH ULM students in Hukumonline’s 2024 favourite-lecturer survey. ### Hukumonline — faculty perspective (verified)

An openly accessible **Hukumonline** feature dated 16 September 2024 attributes a Faculty of Law vision statement to Dr. Achmad Faishal, then Dean of FH ULM. He presents the faculty’s distinctive orientation as legal education connected to local wisdom and wetland resources, supported by a humanistic and innovative learning environment. He also connects curriculum development to legal problems arising from technological change. This is a suitable **faculty perspective**, provided the former-dean status and date are preserved and the entry is not presented as a current institutional position.

Source: [Hukumonline — “Melihat Keunggulan FH Universitas Lambung Mangkurat”](https://www.hukumonline.com/berita/a/melihat-keunggulan-fh-universitas-lambung-mangkurat-lt66e7c1d4321c7/), opened 27 August 2026.

### Quora Indonesia — general alumni perspectives (verified)

The publicly readable answer by **Farikha Ratna Sekar** identifies her as a ULM Faculty of Engineering alumnus. She describes moving between the Banjarmasin and Banjarbaru campuses for academic work, says that this broadened her friendships across programmes, and characterises the teaching atmosphere she encountered as relaxed rather than intimidating. She also frames her later central-government role as a source of pride in ULM. This is suitable as a **ULM general / alumni perspective** and must not be presented as an experience within FH ULM.

The same thread includes an answer by **Erwin Erlangga**, whose public bio identifies him as a 2015 ULM Government Science graduate. He describes meeting peers from varied regional and cultural backgrounds, says that the experience broadened his outlook, and emphasises the solidarity that continued after graduation. This is suitable as a **ULM general / alumni perspective** with an emphasis on peer community.

The thread also includes a response by **Rahmia Amien**, whose visible public bio identifies her as studying Chemistry Education and Chemistry at ULM. She sees the occasional combination of Banjar and Indonesian in teaching as an engaging local-language context that may aid comprehension for many local students. This is suitable as a **ULM general / student perspective**, but it must be explicitly framed as one writer’s observation, not a faculty-wide pedagogic practice.

All three are individual self-published public answers rather than institutional survey data. The page will use short original paraphrases and will not display their profile images, vote counts, or full answer text.

Source: [Quora Indonesia — ULM study-experience thread](https://id.quora.com/Apa-hal-yang-paling-kamu-sukai-dari-berkuliah-di-Universitas-Lambung-Mangkurat), extracted 27 August 2026.
